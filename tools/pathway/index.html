How the design is checked

A smooth degree-seven Bézier curve connects optional straight inlet and outlet sections. It matches position and travel direction with zero curvature at the joins. The section keeps the same dimensions throughout.

Material measurements

Optional Young’s modulus, flexural modulus, tensile strength and flexural strength are recorded separately in MPa. Automatic bending selection prefers recorded flexural modulus, then recorded Young’s modulus, then the editable public reference where available. You can choose explicitly. Values are never averaged. Whole-profile calibration takes precedence for route stiffness.

For PVC-only sections without calibration, bending rigidities are E × section moments. Shear modulus is estimated from Young’s modulus and an editable Poisson’s ratio; flexural modulus is a provisional substitute when Young’s modulus is absent. The initial Poisson’s ratio of 0.38 is an assumption. This combines test-specific bending data with an isotropic shear estimate.

Nominal bending stress is the selected recorded or reference bending modulus × geometric bending strain, even when calibration drives the route. Calibrated rigidities are not divided by an assumed section to infer a material modulus. Strength-only records are saved without producing a stress estimate. Tensile and flexural strengths are separate reference comparisons; neither is an allowable stress or a route constraint. No compression, torsional-stress, pushing or buckling assessment or safety factor is included. Keep test temperature, method, direction and loading-time notes with each record.

Route preference and strain

The 0.1×–10× stiffness slider weights bending and twisting against length. It is a route preference, not a PVC modulus. Solid, hollow and adjustable ribbed sections provide geometric stiffness ratios. The ribbed editor uses a base and T-ribs. Published Expanda and Rotaloc reference heights are kept separate from assumed width and rib dimensions. The shape is an approximation, not manufacturer CAD; product names do not add steel reinforcement.

Sample calibration replaces those ratios with measured effective rigidities in both bending directions and in torsion. The elastic energy estimate assumes reversible beam behaviour and excludes friction, contact and pushing. The pathway and endpoint positions follow the datum marked in the profile preview: area centroid for PVC only, retained PVC centroid for sliding steel, or the elastic centre for no slip. Bending strain is calculated at the actual outline vertices. Area and bending moments, including coupling for asymmetric shapes, are integrated over the material without counting overlaps twice. These describe the entered ideal geometry, not a verified product. Torsional strain and local wall deformation are excluded.

For PVC-only sections, hollow torsion uses a closed thin-wall approximation. Ribbed torsion uses the open-wall estimate J ≈ Σ(length × thickness³ / 3), assuming free warping. Junction effects, warping restraint and shear-centre coupling are omitted. Measured calibration overrides geometric stiffness but assumes independent bending axes; the two bending tests do not measure coupling.

Encapsulated steel and orientation

Steel reinforcement is limited to 91-22ROS and 91-32ROS, inside individual T-ribs. Product applicability and location follow the user’s supplied information. Up to six separate rectangular longitudinal inserts approximate the steel; exact dimensions remain user assumptions. Displaced PVC is removed from area and bending properties. Sliding fit assumes common curvature with independent axial slip and each body bending about its own centroid. No slip assumes perfect load transfer and uses the modulus-weighted elastic centre. Encapsulation alone does not establish bonding. Steel dimensions and the initial 200,000 MPa modulus are editable assumptions. PVC and steel bending strains and nominal stresses are reported separately. No steel yield check is made. Uncalibrated reinforced routes minimise bending only, with torsion unestimated and the geometric twist limit retained. Whole-assembly calibration replaces all calculated rigidities without adding steel twice; it does not identify constituent stress or the neutral axis. Constituent strain and stress still depend on the selected fit and entered moduli.

The no-slip elastic centre is weighted by each material’s modulus and area. Its bending rigidity includes offset-area terms; the sliding assumption sums each body’s own-centroid rigidity without those offset terms. Only separate rectangular inserts are supported; joined U-channels are not represented by treating their walls as independent strips.

Flip profile rotates the complete section 180° about travel, reversing both local width and height directions. It preserves endpoint rolls and sample calibration. Peak absolute elastic bending strain can remain unchanged even when the turned profile changes clearance. Insert positions remain measured from the original PVC lower-left corner. Placement checks require positive PVC cover numerically; this is not a specified manufacturing cover thickness.

Clearance and numerical accuracy

The outer rectangular envelope, positioned about the selected pathway datum and expanded by the selected gap on each side, is checked against boxes, finite vertical cylinders and the workspace. Empty rib channels are not available clearance. Envelope overlap can be reported without actual material contact. The same envelope is screened for non-neighbouring self-contact. Adjacent cells share material and are excluded; local folding is checked separately.

Clearance applies to the piecewise linear sweep between the computed rings. Close obstacle intervals are subdivided down to 0.25 mm along that represented sweep. An overlapping bound without a proven intersection is reported as unresolved. This is not a certified distance tolerance for the underlying smooth curve.

Sample doubling checks stability of path length to 0.05% and peak radius, twist and bending strain to 1%. These are numerical stability targets, not physical accuracy guarantees. The search considers several starting routes and reports the best feasible candidate found.

Physical modelling still needed

Pushing force, buckling, guide friction, contact stress and the initial threading sequence are not simulated. Exact CAD outline import, partial slip and non-rectangular steel sections, multiple waypoint spans and local wall deformation are later extensions. A successful geometric check does not establish those behaviours.

Calibrate from a sample

Measure the actual extrusion at its working temperature, with steel fitted when reinforcement is enabled. Record the fit and assembly condition. These whole-profile tests are an engineering estimate, not a standard-compliance test.

  1. Support the sample at two points without clamping. Seat it and record a baseline.
  2. Add a small load at the midpoint and measure the additional deflection. Repeat with the profile turned 90°. A loading saddle should avoid crushing walls or ribs.
  3. Apply a small known torque and measure additional twist between two marks away from the grips.
  4. Use increments within a repeatable, approximately linear load range. Unload to check recovery. Keep the time after loading consistent.

Bending rigidity = load × span³ ÷ (48 × deflection). Torsional rigidity = torque × gauge length ÷ twist in radians. The app converts N·m to N·mm. Calibration spans may exceed 1,000 mm. The two bending tests use an independent-axis approximation and do not identify coupled bending in an asymmetric section.
